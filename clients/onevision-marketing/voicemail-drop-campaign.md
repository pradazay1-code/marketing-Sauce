# Ringless Voicemail → Callback Campaign
**One Vision Marketing Agency · Isaiah Wright**
Built for GoHighLevel. Voicemail drop → SMS → Email, 10-day sequence.

---

## The strategy in one paragraph

A cold voicemail is not a sales pitch. Its only job is to earn **one callback or
one text reply**. That is the entire conversion event. Every word is spent
buying curiosity and lowering the cost of responding — which is exactly the
Value Equation applied to a 25-second window: raise the perceived payoff of
calling back, and drop the effort of doing it to almost nothing.

The three biggest mistakes, in order:
1. **Too long.** Past 30 seconds people delete without listening.
2. **Sounding like a broadcast.** Polished radio-voice reads as spam instantly.
3. **Asking for a meeting.** Too big an ask from a stranger. Ask for a reply.

## Part 1 — Voicemail scripts

Record these yourself, on your phone, in a quiet room. Do **not** over-rehearse.
A small stumble or a breath makes it sound left-in-the-moment, which is the
entire point. Target **22–28 seconds**.

---

### VM 1 — The Gap *(default cold script — start here)*

> Hey [Name], it's Isaiah with One Vision Marketing, I'm over in Bridgewater.
>
> I was looking at [Business] online and I noticed something that's probably
> costing you calls. It's a quick fix — not a big project.
>
> I didn't want to leave the whole thing in a voicemail. Give me a shout back
> when you get a minute — [phone].
>
> Again, Isaiah, One Vision. Talk soon.

**Why it works:** local placement builds trust, "costing you calls" is their
outcome not your service, "quick fix, not a big project" pre-handles the money
objection, and "didn't want to leave the whole thing" creates the curiosity gap
that earns the callback.

---

### VM 2 — The Specific Observation *(highest reply rate, needs 2 min of research)*

> Hey [Name], Isaiah with One Vision Marketing.
>
> I was on your site and noticed [specific thing — no Google listing / site
> loads slow on mobile / 40 reviews and none answered].
>
> For a [industry] in [town] that usually costs you real calls every month.
>
> I already wrote up what I'd change — happy to just send it over. No charge,
> no pitch. Text me back at [phone] if you want it.
>
> Isaiah, One Vision.

**Why it works:** specificity is proof you actually looked. This one converts at
several times the generic script, and it is worth the research time on any lead
you actually want.

---

### VM 3 — Local Peer Proof

> Hey [Name], Isaiah Wright, One Vision Marketing here in Bridgewater.
>
> We run marketing for a handful of local businesses — a real estate agency in
> Dedham, a tattoo studio down in New Bedford.
>
> I had an idea specific to [Business] I wanted to run past you. Two minutes,
> it's not a sales thing.
>
> [phone]. Thanks [Name].

**Why it works:** named local clients make you real. "Not a sales thing" is
believable *because* you asked for two minutes instead of a meeting.

---

### VM 4 — Re-Engage *(for leads who went quiet)*

> Hey [Name], Isaiah from One Vision — we talked a while back and things went
> quiet, which usually just means the timing was off.
>
> No hard feelings either way. I've got something that might fit better now than
> it did then.
>
> If it's still not the time, just text me "no" and I'll leave you alone for
> good. [phone].

**Why it works:** giving an explicit, easy exit raises total replies. Some of
those replies are "no" — that is fine, it cleans your list. Many are not.

---

### VM 5 — Free Audit *(offer-led, best for warmer lists)*

> Hey [Name], Isaiah with One Vision Marketing.
>
> I do a free marketing audit for local [industry] — takes about fifteen
> minutes, and you keep the breakdown whether you ever work with me or not.
>
> Figured [Business] might get something out of it. Call or text [phone].
>
> Isaiah, One Vision.

## Part 2 — SMS sequence

Fires 3 minutes after the drop, while the voicemail notification is still on
screen. **This is where most of your replies will actually come from.**

### SMS 1 — pairs with VM 1 *(3 min after drop)*
```
Hey [Name] — Isaiah from One Vision, just left you a voicemail.
The thing I spotted on your site is about a 5 min fix.
Want me to just text you what it is?
```
The ask is "want me to text you" — a near-zero-effort yes.

### SMS 2 — pairs with VM 2 *(3 min after drop)*
```
[Name] — Isaiah, One Vision Marketing. Left you a VM.
Short version: I wrote up what I'd change on [Business]'s
setup. Free, no pitch. Want it?
```

### SMS 3 — the either/or *(day 3, no reply)*
```
Hey [Name], following up once. Should I send that breakdown
over, or is now just not the time? Either answer's fine.
```
Making "no" easy raises reply rate. Hormozi's point: the enemy is silence, not
rejection.

### SMS 4 — closing the file *(day 8, final)*
```
[Name] — closing your file so I stop showing up in your phone.
If it's ever worth a look, I'm here. — Isaiah, One Vision
```
Consistently the highest-reply message in a cold sequence. People respond to a
door closing.

## Part 3 — The email

Cold email lives or dies on three things: it looks personally typed, it makes
one small ask, and it is short enough to read on a lock screen.

### Subject lines *(lowercase, no punctuation — looks human, not sent)*
- `quick one about [Business]`
- `noticed something on your site`
- `[Business] — one thing`
- `question for you [Name]`

### Email 1 — The Observation *(day 1, after VM + SMS)*

```
Hey [Name],

Isaiah here — I run One Vision Marketing out of Bridgewater.

I was looking at [Business] and noticed [specific observation].
For a [industry] around here, that usually costs somewhere in the
range of a few calls a month. Not a huge project to fix.

I already wrote up what I'd change — about a page.

Want me to send it? Just reply "yes" and it's yours. No pitch
attached, and I won't chase you if it isn't useful.

— Isaiah
One Vision Marketing
Onevisionmarketingagency.com
```

**No links, no attachment, no calendar.** In a first email every one of those
drops deliverability and raises friction. The only ask is the word "yes."

### Email 2 — The Bump *(day 5, no reply)*

```
[Name] — bumping this once in case it got buried.

Still happy to send the breakdown over. One word reply and
it's on its way.

— Isaiah
```

Three lines. Sent as a reply on the same thread, never a new one.

### Email 3 — The Close-Out *(day 10, final)*

```
Hey [Name],

I'll assume the timing isn't right and leave it there — I'd
rather be useful than persistent.

If [Business] ever wants a second set of eyes on the marketing,
you know where to find me. No expiry on the offer.

— Isaiah
One Vision Marketing
```

## Part 4 — GoHighLevel workflow

**Trigger:** Tag added → `cold-outreach` (or lead enters your Cold pipeline stage)

| Step | Wait | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 min | **Voicemail Drop** — VM 1 recording |
| 2 | 3 min | **SMS** — SMS 1 |
| 3 | 1 day | **Email** — Email 1 |
| 4 | 2 days | **SMS** — SMS 3 (either/or) |
| 5 | 2 days | **Email** — Email 2 (reply on thread) |
| 6 | 3 days | **SMS** — SMS 4 (closing your file) |
| 7 | 2 days | **Email** — Email 3, then remove tag |

### Build it in GHL

1. **Automation → Workflows → Create Workflow → Start from Scratch**
2. **Add Trigger** → *Contact Tag Added* → tag `cold-outreach`
3. **Add Action** → *Ringless Voicemail* → upload your VM 1 recording
   - Requires a Twilio number connected under Settings → Phone Numbers
4. **Add Action** → *Wait* → 3 minutes
5. **Add Action** → *Send SMS* → paste SMS 1
6. Continue down the table above with Wait + Send actions

### The exit condition — do not skip this

Add at the very top of the workflow:

**Trigger** → *Customer Replied* → **Action:** Remove from this workflow +
Add tag `warm-lead` + Send internal notification to your phone.

Without this, someone who replies "yes, send it" keeps getting your
"closing your file" text four days later. That kills more deals than a weak
script does.

### Send-window settings

Under workflow **Settings**:
- **Send window:** 9:00 AM – 7:00 PM
- **Timezone:** Contact's timezone
- **Skip:** Sundays

Best drop times for local trades and services: **Tuesday–Thursday, 10am–12pm or
2pm–4pm.** Avoid Monday morning and Friday afternoon.

## Part 5 — Recording the voicemail

Do this once and reuse it. Fifteen minutes of effort, used a thousand times.

**Setup**
- Phone's built-in Voice Memos, in a quiet room with soft furnishings
- Phone about 8 inches away, slightly off to the side so you don't pop your P's
- Stand up — it changes your voice

**Delivery**
- Speak slightly *slower* than feels natural
- Smile while you talk. It's audible.
- Say the number twice, second time slower
- Do not read word-for-word. Learn the beats and talk.

**Take 4 or 5 and pick the one that sounds least rehearsed.** The imperfect take
almost always outperforms the polished one.

**Export:** MP3 or WAV, under 30 seconds, then upload to GHL under
Marketing → Voicemails (or attach directly in the workflow action).

## Part 6 — Numbers to expect and what to track

Realistic for cold local B2B:

| Metric | Expect | Fix if below |
|---|---|---|
| Voicemail delivered | 85–95% | Bad numbers — clean the list |
| Callback rate | 2–5% | Script too long or too salesy |
| SMS reply rate | 8–15% | Ask is too big — shrink it |
| Email reply rate | 3–8% | Subject line, or too much in the body |
| Reply → booked call | 30–50% | Follow up faster; speed is everything |

**Track in GHL:** Reporting → Attribution. Watch reply rate per step and cut
whichever step produces nothing after 200 sends.

**The single highest-leverage variable is speed to response.** Replying to an
inbound text within 5 minutes converts multiples better than replying in an
hour. This is exactly what the AI callback bot in `directives/ai_callback_bot.md`
was built for — wire it to this workflow and the response is instant.

## Compliance — read this once

Ringless voicemail sits in genuinely unsettled legal territory. Several courts
have treated RVM as a "call" under the TCPA, which would mean it needs the same
consent as a dialed call. It is not settled law, and it is worth knowing before
you scale volume.

Practical risk reduction:
- **Business numbers, not personal cell numbers.** B2B outreach to a listed
  business line is materially lower risk than B2C to a mobile.
- **Scrub against the National DNC Registry** before any push.
- **Honor opt-outs immediately** — GHL does this automatically on STOP, but make
  sure the workflow's exit condition is actually wired.
- **Keep records** of where each number came from.
- **Massachusetts** has its own telemarketing rules on top of federal. Worth a
  one-time look before you run high volume.

None of this is legal advice — it's the operational shortlist. If this becomes a
meaningful channel for you, an hour with an attorney who knows TCPA is cheap
insurance.
