The Founder

01

Background

Isaiah Wright is the founder and operator of One Vision Marketing Agency, based in Bridgewater, Massachusetts. He holds a B.A. in Marketing from Bridgewater State University, and his formal training shows up in how he runs the business — he thinks in positioning, offer construction, and measurable funnels rather than in deliverables and hours.

Before building the agency into its current form, he conducted marketing research for RECNA (Regional Economic Center for New Americans) in Bridgewater, helping the organization expand its reach and community impact. That work is a useful lens on him: his earliest professional marketing experience was in service of a community organization, not a corporate brand, and the community-first orientation has persisted into how he structures client relationships today.

What Distinguishes Him

Most small marketing agencies are staffed by generalists who outsource the technical work. Isaiah does the opposite — he builds the infrastructure himself. He has developed a custom CRM application, automated lead-generation tooling, an AI-powered content visual generator, and an AI voice callback system that phones new leads within sixty seconds of form submission. These are not tools he resells; they are tools he wrote.

The practical consequence is that his agency's cost structure and capability set look different from a comparable shop. He can offer software, content production, and strategy inside a single monthly retainer because the underlying systems are automated rather than manual.

"You're not hiring an agency. You're hiring a partner."

This line appears in his sales materials, and it accurately describes the delivery model — clients work directly with him, not an account manager.

Working Style

  • Systems-first. When a task recurs, he builds a script or a template for it rather than repeating manual work. His operating repository is organized around reusable directives and execution scripts.
  • Ships fast, iterates in public. He prefers a working draft today over a perfect draft next week, then refines through rapid cycles.
  • Tests like an end user. He checks deliverables on his own phone before they reach a client and pushes back hard when something renders incorrectly — a discipline most agencies skip.
  • Receptive to honest counsel. When told a goal was unrealistic — for example, ranking for a head keyword dominated by billion-dollar competitors — he adjusted strategy rather than insisting.
  • Bootstrapper's economics. He consistently seeks the free or low-cost path first, and scales spend only against demonstrated return.

The Company

02

Positioning

One Vision Marketing Agency is a full-service marketing partnership for small and mid-sized businesses. Its central thesis is consolidation: most small businesses are paying separately for a CRM, an email platform, a website builder, a scheduler, a review tool, and a social scheduler — spending between $500 and $1,600 per month on tools that do not communicate with one another and that nobody is accountable for.

One Vision replaces that stack with a single system, a single invoice, and a single accountable partner.

One Vision. One Team. Real Growth.

Brand Evolution

The company previously operated as Aventis Marketing and has since consolidated under the One Vision brand. A parallel venture, AventisAI, was developed as a white-label software play — a rebranded all-in-one platform sold as a monthly subscription, modeled on reselling GoHighLevel's SaaS tier at 80–95% margin. The business plan for that venture is fully documented, and its underlying logic — build once, deliver to many — informs how the agency is structured today.

Sales Methodology

Isaiah's sales approach is built explicitly on Alex Hormozi's offer and closing frameworks, applied with unusual rigor for an agency of this size.

  • The Value Equation. Offers are constructed to maximize dream outcome and perceived likelihood of success while minimizing time delay and client effort. Fast onboarding and done-for-you setup are treated as competitive weapons, not conveniences.
  • Grand Slam Offer construction. The value stack is itemized line by line with individual dollar figures, then anchored against a materially lower price.
  • The CLOSER framework. Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the value, Explain next steps, Reinforce with a guarantee — used as the actual structure of discovery and closing calls.
  • Risk reversal. The guarantee is positioned prominently rather than buried, on the reasoning that a larger guarantee closes more business.

Lead Generation for the Agency Itself

He applies the same systems thinking to his own pipeline: cold email at scale, LinkedIn outreach, free marketing audits as a lead magnet, short-form video content, Facebook group prospecting with a value-first posture, referral incentives, and local partnerships with accountants and business consultants.

Notably, his stated policy for group and community outreach is to lead with genuine help and never with a pitch — commenting publicly with useful advice first, and only moving to a direct conversation after the other party engages.

How He Operates

05

The Operating Architecture

Isaiah runs his agency on a deliberately structured three-layer model that separates intent from execution:

  • Directive layer. Standard operating procedures written in plain language — one document per recurring task, defining goals, inputs, tools, outputs, and edge cases.
  • Orchestration layer. Decision-making and routing — reading the directive, selecting the right tool, handling errors, and updating the procedure with what was learned.
  • Execution layer. Deterministic scripts that do the actual work — API calls, data processing, file operations, image generation.

The reasoning he applies is straightforwardly quantitative: at 90% accuracy per step, a five-step process succeeds only 59% of the time. Pushing complexity into deterministic code rather than repeated human judgment is how he keeps quality consistent as volume grows.

A Notable Detail

His delivery standard for client websites and documents is that every file must be fully self-contained — all styling, scripts, and graphics embedded inline, no external requests. This is not an aesthetic preference. It is a reliability decision: a file that depends on a content delivery network will eventually fail to load on a client's phone, on a restricted network, or offline. He has tested for and corrected exactly this failure mode.

The Client Experience Model

In September 2026 he launched a monthly community update program — a recurring briefing sent to every client covering what is being rolled out that month. The first edition covered expanded SEO keyword research, new CRM automation features, and the introduction of a monthly giveaway in which the top-performing client receives a full month of service at no cost.

The design intent is worth noting: the program reframes the client base as a community rather than a list of accounts, creates a recurring reason for positive contact that is not an invoice or an upsell, and introduces a light competitive dynamic that rewards engagement.

Where It Is Headed

  • Productization. Continued movement from custom service delivery toward repeatable, software-supported packages that scale without proportional labor.
  • Software as a second revenue line. The AI callback system and CRM tooling are built to be resold to clients as standalone add-ons at high margin.
  • Content-led inbound. An acknowledged priority to build a personal brand through consistent short-form video, on the reasoning that clients hire people rather than logos.
  • Local market depth. Rather than competing for national head terms, the strategy targets winnable local and long-tail positions across specific Massachusetts markets.

Summary Assessment

Isaiah Wright operates less like a traditional agency owner and more like a solo technical founder who happens to sell marketing. He writes his own software, structures offers using tested frameworks, prices aggressively against a stacked value proposition, and builds systems that let one person deliver what would normally require a small team. The company is early, deliberately lean, and constructed to scale through automation rather than headcount.
